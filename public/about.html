Three ways to search

  • Type a postcode

    With or without the space, in any case. Every business within 1, 2 or 5 miles appears, nearest first.

  • Use your location

    One tap finds what’s around you right now. Your position is only used for that search, and it isn’t stored.

  • Press and hold the map

    Planning a trip? Hold anywhere on the map, or right-click on a computer, to see ratings around that spot.

What the ratings mean

Council food safety officers inspect how food is handled, stored and prepared, how clean the premises are, and how food safety is managed. The business gets a rating based on what they find on the day.

Every pin and badge in FoodCheck shows the number or result, not just a colour, so ratings are clear for everyone.

Built for deciding where to eat

  • Filter what matters

    Only show places rated 4 or better, or only takeaways, pubs or cafés. Choose 1, 2 or 5 miles.

  • Map and list together

    Tap a pin to jump to the business in the list, or tap the list to find it on the map. Expand the map to full screen whenever you like.

  • Every place, page by page

    Busy areas have thousands of businesses. Results come 50 at a time, nearest first, so you can page through them all.

  • The official record

    Each listing links to its page on the Food Standards Agency website, with the inspection date.

  • Your recent searches

    Places you’ve checked are a tap away under the search box and on the History page. You can turn this off.

  • Light or dark

    Match your phone, or choose light or dark mode in Settings. Large, high-contrast badges work for everyone.

Put it on your home screen

FoodCheck works like an app. Once installed, it opens full screen, and your recent results are there even without a signal.

  1. iPhone or iPad: open FoodCheck in Safari, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.
  2. Android: open FoodCheck in Chrome, tap the ⋮ menu, then Install app or Add to Home screen.
  3. Computer: in Chrome or Edge, select the install icon in the address bar.

Private by design

There are no accounts, no cookies, no adverts and no tracking. Your settings and search history stay on your own device, and you can clear them at any time.

Questions

Where does the information come from?

From the Food Standards Agency’s open data. Local councils inspect businesses and publish the results there. FoodCheck shows them unchanged, alongside a link to each official record.

How up to date are the ratings?

A rating reflects what the inspector found on the day of the last inspection, and FoodCheck shows that date. Businesses are inspected more or less often depending on their risk, and they can ask for a re-inspection after making improvements. Always check the official record before making an important decision.

What does “Awaiting inspection” or “Exempt” mean?

“Awaiting inspection” usually means a new or newly registered business that hasn’t been inspected yet. “Exempt” businesses are very low risk, such as a newsagent selling only packaged food, so they aren’t rated.

Why isn’t a business on the map?

A few businesses have no map location in the official data, such as mobile caterers or market stalls. They still appear in the list, marked “No map location”.

Is FoodCheck run by the Food Standards Agency?

No. FoodCheck is an independent, free tool built on the Food Standards Agency’s public data. It isn’t affiliated with the FSA or any council.

Does it cost anything?

No. There’s nothing to buy, no subscription and no sign-up.
